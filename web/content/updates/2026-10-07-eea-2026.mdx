The European Elasmobranch Association conference, EEA 2026, runs from 6 to 8 October. We are following eight abstracts from the booklet, across two themes: depredation and human–shark conflict, and deterrents and bycatch mitigation. Times on the research page are in Japan Standard Time, and the results are as reported in the abstracts.

## What stands out

- Two independent trials of electrical deterrents reported depredation reductions of about 60%: a ten-year review from the University of Western Australia, and a randomised trial of the RPELX device at Cocos Keeling Islands that lowered the probability of a depredation event by 63%.
- A deterrent can also scare off the target catch. In the SharkGuard trial on tuna longliners in New Caledonia, the distance between device and hook decided the outcome: 60 cm above the hook cut shark bycatch by 46.6% with no significant loss of target catch, while 40 cm cut it by 88.7% but cost 19.4% of the target catch.
- Devices designed and adjusted with fishers are more likely to be adopted. Workshops in the Azores corrected assumptions about depth, season and effort, and those corrections changed a vulnerability assessment.

## What it means for Step 1

None of these trials measured what happens to a hooked tuna on a longline off Kesennuma. That is the gap the logger is built for: count every hook-up and every strike before anyone claims a deterrent works here.
